## Purpose
Identify missed-appointment patterns across eight clinics, estimate no-show risk the evening before an appointment, and classify follow-up notes for operational action.

## Recommendation (update with actual model and Gemini outputs)
Prioritise evening-before calls for appointments with high predicted risk, particularly long-lead bookings: the 31+ day group had a 60.5% no-show rate (26/43), compared with 16.2% (63/390) for 1–7 days. Overall, 218 of 990 appointments were missed (22.0%). Start with a manageable daily call capacity, monitor outcomes and consider reminder delivery quality. The selected logistic model had training CV ROC-AUC 0.774 and a threshold of 0.35, flagging 49 of 198 held-out bookings. Manual review of the first 10 notes is supplied separately; genuine Gemini outputs remain pending and must not be represented as executed. These are associations, not causal estimates.

## Model selection and threshold
Training CV ROC-AUC: logistic regression 0.7737; depth-3 tree 0.6784; untuned forest 0.7466; tuned forest 0.7673. Logistic regression wins. Out-of-fold training threshold 0.50: precision 0.5753, recall 0.2485, F1 0.3471, 73 flagged; threshold 0.35: precision 0.5130, recall 0.4675, F1 0.4892, 154 flagged. Select 0.35 by higher training F1. Held-out test: precision 0.5714, recall 0.5714, ROC-AUC 0.7889, 49 flagged among 198. Operational calling capacity must determine the real threshold.

## Limitations
1. The first 10 follow-up notes are illustrative and cannot represent clinic-wide causes.
2. Medians, quartiles and clipping thresholds were calculated before the train/test split, causing limited leakage; a deployed pipeline would learn them on training data only.
3. Random row splitting may put the same patient in both train and test; a patient-grouped, chronological validation is preferable before deployment.
4. Observational data cannot establish that reminders cause fewer no-shows; threshold selection must reflect real staff capacity and the cost of unused appointments.

## Final integrated recommendation (data-backed, with manual note evidence)
Of 990 appointments, 218 were missed (22.0%). Bookings made 31+ days ahead had 26 misses out of 43 (60.5%), versus 63 out of 390 (16.2%) for 1–7 days. A training-only cross-validation comparison selected logistic regression (mean ROC-AUC 0.774), with the 0.35 threshold preferred on training F1. On the held-out set its ROC-AUC was 0.789 and it flagged 49 of 198 bookings, with precision and recall both 57.1%. Recommend a targeted evening-before call queue for high-risk bookings, beginning with long-lead appointments and missed reminder delivery, while measuring actual contact capacity, attendance and staff workload. In the ten manually reviewed follow-up notes, two explicitly cite forgotten/no reminder, two transport, two work schedules, one cost, one illness, and two other/unknown (one blank). These small descriptive counts do not establish population-level cause or treatment effectiveness.
